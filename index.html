Counts show statewide popular-vote winners across 13 elections. “Red” and “blue” here describe presidential results, not state government control. Maine and Nebraska can split electoral votes by district.
Why the numbers matter
Each state gets electoral votes equal to its House seats plus two senators. Winning more states or more nationwide popular votes does not necessarily win the presidency. A candidate needs a majority of electoral votes — normally 270 of 538. Washington, D.C. has 3 electoral votes and is not one of the 50 states in this game.
