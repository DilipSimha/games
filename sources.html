A little context

What the games show.

Data snapshot: October 30, 2026. Facts are learning summaries, with dates and conventions made explicit below.

World Explorer

The quiz includes 193 United Nations member states plus the Holy See / Vatican City and Palestine: 195 entries. Dependencies and other territories can appear as background map context but are not quiz answers. Antarctica has no sovereign countries and is not a quiz option.

Each country is placed in one of six quiz continents, primarily using Natural Earth's classification. Transcontinental countries can have land in more than one continent; their quiz grouping is a convention. North America includes Central America and the Caribbean. Oceania includes Australia and Pacific island countries.

Maps carry no country numbers or name labels. Small countries have a highlighted locator dot; outlines and dots are not always to scale. Zoom in for tiny countries. The map is a simplified educational depiction; boundaries, recognition and sovereignty can be disputed.

Capitals and seats of government sometimes differ; multiple seats are called out in the reveal. Equatorial Guinea's capital was updated to Ciudad de la Paz following its January 2026 decree. Indonesia is shown as Jakarta with its planned Nusantara transition noted. Language and currency fields are selected source-dataset entries, not exhaustive descriptions of each country's people. Areas are rounded dataset values in square kilometres.

Sources: UN member states · UN non-member observer states · mledoze country dataset (ODbL) · Natural Earth boundaries (public domain) · Equatorial Guinea capital decree · Indonesia capital transition.

India Explorer

Includes 28 states and 8 union territories. Capital lists include seasonal capitals and legislative seats where relevant. Formation years are key constitutional or reorganisation milestones; they do not imply that every region was created from nothing in that year. The history cards give the more useful context.

Sri Vijaya Puram is the current name of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands' capital, formerly Port Blair. Andhra Pradesh's capital is shown as Amaravati. The areas of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh are omitted because territorial claims and administrative-area conventions vary.

District geometries were grouped by state/UT and simplified for this game. They are educational map outlines, not survey or legal boundaries.

Sources: Ministry of Home Affairs · Know India — states and union territories · India Code — reorganisation laws · Andaman and Nicobar administration · Andhra Pradesh capital region · Uttarakhand capitals · Jammu and Kashmir · Puducherry history · India map source.

U.S. Presidents

The game follows the official numbered presidencies: 47 through the snapshot date. Grover Cleveland and Donald Trump each appear in two separate entries because they returned after nonconsecutive terms. Consecutive reelections are grouped into one presidency. Years are calendar-year ranges, not full years served. Facts about deaths, resignations and succession explain why some presidencies were short.

Sources: White House Historical Association — timeline and linked biographies · USAGov — presidency · National Archives presidential libraries.

India's Prime Ministers

The game lists 18 successive continuous tenures, including Gulzarilal Nanda's two acting periods. These are not official prime-minister ordinal numbers. Consecutive reelections are grouped, while a return after another leader produces a new card. The 1996 Vajpayee government is remembered as a thirteen-day government, but his caretaker tenure continued until the June 1 succession; the dates follow the Prime Minister's Office.

Each card lists presidents whose terms overlap that prime-minister tenure. Acting presidents during vacancies are identified; brief temporary delegation of presidential functions is not tracked. Before the republic began on January 26, 1950, India had governors-general, not presidents. Nehru's card explains this distinction.

Prime ministers head the government; presidents are heads of state. Naming a president alongside a prime minister does not mean the president directed that government's policies.

Sources: Prime Minister's Office — former prime ministers · Current prime minister · President of India — former presidents and biographies · Current president.

The original USA states game

Population: 2020 resident Census. Area includes land and water; density uses land. House seats and electoral votes follow the 2024–2028 allocation. Red and blue show the statewide popular-vote winner in the 2024 presidential election, not current state-government control. Maine and Nebraska can split electoral votes. Each admission story names the president at statehood, which can differ from the president when the U.S. acquired the territory.
